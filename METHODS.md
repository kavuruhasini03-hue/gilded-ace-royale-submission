# Dashboard Royale methods

## Player concentration

The population consists of distinct players in `player_period` during January through June 2025. Sum each player's tracked theo, tracked coin-in and tracked gaming net across the six months before ranking. Rank by descending tracked theo, breaking ties by player ID for reproducibility.

`gold_lorenz` accumulates the actual dollars at each rank. `gold_concentration` takes the last whole-player rank whose cumulative population share is at or below 1% or 10%, using integer comparisons. Both dollar shares use the same tracked-theo-ranked group. Divide cumulative theo by total tracked theo and cumulative coin-in by total tracked coin-in. Group net is the sum of tracked payouts minus tracked wagers from the player perspective. A negative number is a net loss before comps and other spending.

## Comp reinvestment

The headline line in `gold_comp_week` divides economic redemption cost by tracked theo. The secondary line divides the same cost by all active-floor theo, including uncarded play. Both are ratios of weekly sums. Weekly tracked theo comes from carded session segments, which carry a gaming date. Floor theo comes from installed machine-day rows in `prepared_daily_view`.

Redemptions are assigned to gaming dates by the UTC calendar interval, inclusive at the start and exclusive at the end. Weeks start Monday. The first and last weeks cover only gaming dates inside the release window. Economic cost comes from the redemption ledger and is counted once. Face value is used only for the points-per-face-dollar measure. Zero or missing theo gives a null rate.

## Gaming-time alignment

Silver event tables retain source timestamps and assign gaming dates using the calendar's UTC intervals. Rows outside the release gaming calendar have a null gaming date. Gaming-hour labels count local wall-clock hours from 06:00. UTC hour keys preserve the identity of each elapsed hour.

Session segments carry a gaming date and are aggregated before other facts are joined. The session hourly table attributes each segment to its start hour because the release does not supply its individual spin timestamps. Use the released `machine_hour` meters for exact carded spin and wager totals by clock hour.

## 4-L bridge and verdicts

Rates use theo divided by observed available hours. The config benchmark is the median of machine-config rates. The floor benchmark is the median of machine rates. The zone factor is the median machine ratio to its config benchmark, calculated using the machine's days in that zone. For a machine with more than one config in a zone, first take its median config-relative rate. These full-window benchmarks are fixed references. Date, zone and machine filters change exposure, dollars and aggregate rates without re-estimating the references.

Daily downtime is scheduled minus available seconds. Allocate daily downtime across elapsed machine hours in proportion to hourly unavailable seconds, then weight each hour by floor occupancy at that gaming hour relative to overall floor occupancy. Both occupancy rates use summed occupied seconds divided by summed available seconds. The weighted hours are valued at the machine's zone-adjusted config rate. Direct ticket costs are available separately and are not a theo loss.

Bridge potential is the floor peer rate on available hours plus estimated leak. Subtract leak, add signed location and lineup, and add the machine-specific residual to reach theo. Machine-specific is the difference between actual theo and zone-adjusted config expectation on available hours. Amounts are stored to four decimal places, with machine-specific defined after the other components are rounded so the bridge adds exactly. Add actual house win minus theo to reach actual house win. The Floor Health benchmark is separate and adds the best-zone opportunity to theo and leak.

Luck variance is sum of squared bets times payout multiplier variance, converted from cents squared to dollars squared. Sum daily variances before taking a square root. Divide aggregate luck by that standard deviation. The 90% machine-effect interval uses 400 deterministic bootstrap replicates of observed floor days, holding the peer references fixed. The adjusted list requires 60 floor days and ranks by the interval's upper bound. An absolute luck z of at least 3 routes to inspection. Otherwise choose the largest adverse dollar component, in location, leak, lineup, then luck order for ties. With no adverse component, use LUCK for a neutral or favorable result. Replacement can take precedence when exposure is at least 60 floor days, the CI upper bound is negative, and the best 12-month replacement option pays back within the horizon. The July view is a budget screen. When the CI upper bound is negative but the materiality gate fails, show the dominant other suit and flag the shortfall as immaterial.

Replacement assumes a peer-rate machine and projects the observed signed machine-specific gap per calendar day, preserving the historical exposure rate. For each option and horizon, operating days are the horizon minus catalog lead days, floored at zero. Budget cost is quantity multiplied by the sum of fixed cost and the daily charge for those operating days. The July lease therefore costs $3,380 and accrues 16 days of uplift. The 12-month view selects the lease or purchase with the highest expected net benefit over 365 days. Net-benefit intervals transform the bootstrap machine-effect interval through the selected option and subtract its costs. Payback starts at the order date, includes the lead, and is reported in 365/12-day months. The estimates assume full realization of the peer gap after lead time and exclude further installation disruption. For Phase 5, estimate net benefit after full costs and implementation downtime at each horizon, compare against no action, and allow unspent budget. The six-month floor downtime estimate is $98,064 of theo, rather than recoverable cash.

## Competing explanations

The walkway difference-in-differences compares Back Forty with all other zones on pooled coin-in per installed machine-day. The closure window is assigned using UTC interval overlap. The pre-trend check regresses the daily Back Forty minus control gap on elapsed days and reports a conventional OLS standard error. The comparison is descriptive and can be affected by coincident events and spillover to control zones. Weekly traffic splits weeks that span phases. It divides observed entries by minutes with a recorded count and positive coverage, converted to sensor-hours. The control pools all other zones. Coverage share is relative to reported sensor time, and crossings are not unique visitors.

The regression-to-the-mean check follows the full-window GM bottom ten and ranks machines within each half by house win per available hour. Low raw totals can reflect limited floor exposure. Fault groupings report counts and mean response times, with manufacturer and gaming-hour exposure measured in machine-hours and shift exposure in roster clock hours. Tickets opened in a roster gap are shown separately.

The [deployment script](scripts/deploy_phase2.py) can upload the release and run the same SQL from the command line. Its `--skip-bronze` option uses the Delta tables already present.

## Phase 2 re-check

The bank screen compares the adjusted ten with the 308 machines eligible for adjusted ranking, grouped by each machine's last observed bank. The exact null is a simple random sample of ten without replacement. The exact calculation counts all ten-machine selections that contain at most two machines from each bank, divides by all ten-machine selections, and takes the complement. The test accounts for scanning all banks. It does not control for shared positions, configuration, or zone, and a bank move would test the position hypothesis rather than prove it.

## Q2–Q5 analysis

Event comparisons use the same local weekday and gaming month on days without listed events. Gaps are descriptive and overlapping events can share days. Weather is joined by the calendar label of the gaming day's start and kept as context. LVCVA is monthly public context only. Weekly walkway rates and the zone-hour traffic view use counter coverage rather than treating outages as zero entries.

The downtime detail allocates each machine-day's rounded leak across its gaming hours in proportion to weighted downtime. This preserves the daily leak total and supports comparisons by month, zone, manufacturer and hour. Ticket wait and work time remain separate from machine exposure.

Player deciles are fixed by Jan–Jun tracked theo, with player ID breaking ties. The monthly segment view counts player-months. The window outcome view counts each player once. Player net and luck use the player sign convention, and contribution uses the supplied POS and comp accounting.

Comp activity means positive recorded coin-in during the period. Redemption counts include all qualifying redemption events, including those by players without tracked play in the same period. The change date minimizes face-dollar-weighted squared error in a two-level daily points-per-face-dollar fit with at least 28 days on both sides. Before and after rates pool 28 days each and compare tier and region as potential controls. The 90% interval resamples four seven-day blocks within each side. It is conditional on the selected date and describes the observed rate shift rather than the causal effect of a policy.


## Phase 3 re-check

Zone and floor monthly daily rates divide by all release gaming days in the month. The monthly demand bridge uses common weekday references from non-event days, a Back Forty closure allowance based on the descriptive DiD gap times exposed machine-days, and a listed-event deviation from the weekday reference after removing the closure allowance. Theo for the closure is imputed at observed floor theo hold. Other absorbs everything left. The bridge reconciles arithmetically and does not identify independent causal effects. January and June have no closure exposure, so the endpoint change has zero direct closure component even though March and April carry a closure estimate.

High-Limit coin-in per installed machine-day factors into scheduled hours per machine-day, availability, occupancy of available hours, paid spins per occupied hour and dollars per paid spin. Occupancy is measured in time, while the spins term captures the pace of tracked and untracked floor play combined.

Player outcomes exclude zero coin-in from the winner, loser and exactly-zero categories. Comp behavior uses four seven-day blocks on each side of May 1. Visits are recorded visit starts per week. Player-normalized redemption and theo rates pool weekly active-player exposures, so a player active in two weeks contributes twice. Earn-rate ratios use total recorded award points over tracked coin-in or theo. The ledger starts with zero points balance, and the liability view tracks outstanding points in units of points without assigning a dollar obligation. The bootstrap intervals are descriptive and conditional on the date, with only four blocks per side. With no untreated tracked-player cohort, the historical shift does not establish causal lift for A-COMP-PILOT. Uncarded meters offer a floor-level control as described below. A pilot would need a prospective randomized holdout and predefined cost and behavior outcomes.

## Phase 4 forecast

The baseline is mean daily floor coin-in from January 1 through April 30. The alternative fits weekday intercepts and two binary flags for an announced event and a federal holiday. Event eligibility is strictly earlier than the gaming date. The flag coefficients use a fixed ridge penalty of 1 after centering within weekday. Weekday effects and the baseline are unpenalized. No weather, visitation, realized play or holdout outcome enters either fit.

The job materializes both predictions from frozen coefficients before joining actuals. It scores the 61 May–June gaming days using mean absolute dollar error and mean absolute percentage error. Residuals are actual minus prediction. Weekday residuals show recurring bias, while training-hour theo per available machine-hour indicates when downtime is most costly. This timing screen does not price or authorize a technician shift.

The bundle produces the Gold model inputs, parameters, predictions and metrics. After a run, use [the logging script](scripts/log_forecast.py) to record both frozen fits and their holdout metrics in MLflow.

## Phase 4 re-check

Outstanding points are valued using the trailing 28-day realized face dollars and economic cost per redeemed point. The snapshot is as of gaming-day close. The May 28 counterfactual adds the prior 28 days' net accrual to the April 30 balance and values it at the April 30 rate. Excess valuation separates excess point accrual at the old rate and repricing of the full closing balance. This is an exposure at realized redemption mix and full use. Breakage, future mix and timing are unmodeled.

The natural-control analysis uses weeks anchored on May 1 and compares four complete weeks on each side. The control is uncarded meters. Dollar and percentage DiD use proportional control growth to preserve the groups' different base sizes. The percentage-point share change uses pooled weekly meter totals. The pre-trend regresses complete pre-change weeks' log carded-to-uncarded ratio and carded share on elapsed weeks with conventional standard errors. The interval resamples complete weeks within each side. The tracked-theo-per-player sensitivity applies the uncarded theo growth factor to the baseline player rate because there is no uncarded player denominator. Card switching, spillovers and differences in customer mix still limit causal attribution.

The zone bridge uses the same weekday, event and closure adjustments as the floor bridge. It applies equivalent weekday and event standardization to occupied hours and spins. Closure-hour and closure-spin allowances use the observed Back Forty hourly and spin mix to translate the coin-in allowance. The standardized levels add each zone's common weekday reference to the residual for coin-in, occupied hours and spins. A log-mean decomposition then allocates the exact residual change to occupied hours, dollars per spin and spins per occupied hour. These are standardized effects. The separate post-closure Back Forty comparison against January is descriptive and is not added on top of the residual.

The High-Limit visit test freezes the top decile from January–April tracked theo among players with positive coin-in. It counts each recorded visit once at its first High-Limit play, estimates tracked machine time from session segment spans, and compares January–April with June. Intervals resample seven-day blocks within each period, weighting partial blocks by their days. Session spans are a proxy for attributable seat time. Floor meter occupancy includes uncarded play.

## Phase 5 action economics

The decision compares every catalog action with keeping the budget. A scenario's net is modeled incremental theo minus catalog cash cost and implementation disruption valued at the training profile's 10th-percentile machine-hour. It is not a cash-flow forecast. Unknown benefits receive zero base credit and retain a stated measurement rule. The 12-month view assumes one July action unless ongoing cost is explicit, as with leases and repeated technician packages. The maintenance case assumes a single sweep with no more than 90 operating days of effect.

For technician coverage, take repair tickets opened during historical grave roster shifts or during an eight-hour Saturday shift starting at 18:00 local. Keep zero-wait tickets in the fault count and clip observed waits to shift hours. Price each overlap at the forecast's Jan–Apr weekday and gaming-hour theo per available machine-hour. Per-shift upside is faults per shift times average reducible wait per fault times the wait-weighted hourly rate. Recover 25%, 50% or 75% of that overlap in the scenarios, and report full elimination as a ceiling. Saturday-only coverage pays for the full package and gets credit only on eligible Saturdays. Both options use the same catalog lead. Twelve-month continuous staffing uses enough 30-day packages to cover the operating window.

The Hotel Corridor · Bank 40 case assumes a scenario share of the targeted bank's net machine-specific shortfall can be recovered without losing theo elsewhere. Verify destination and displacement before relying on it. Individual replacement ranges use the bootstrap interval for the full peer gap. The GM basket sums interval endpoints as scenarios, without treating the result as a joint confidence interval. The control-adjusted comp result is insufficient to book incremental lift for a pilot or rollout. Information-only actions are visible with their costs and an unpriced benefit.

The action board includes a ±30% downtime-value sensitivity. The cage-slip assertions enforce the July budget, three-type maximum, catalog quantities, conflicts on machine targets, comp policy incompatibilities, and the exact lead-adjusted July cost formula.

### Information value revision

A July authorization can buy information whose value arrives after July. The July budget charges the full catalog cost; the information scenarios subtract that cost and any inspection disruption. They use a stated probability times the conditional decision value. The probabilities and scenario intervals are planning assumptions, not measured frequencies.

Inspection screens the full set of machines with valid z-scores using two-sided normal tails and Bonferroni adjustment. That tail probability is not the probability the finding is luck. To estimate the latter, the base prior expects one genuinely shifted machine across the tested floor, with a shifted-machine effect prior of normal standard deviation 2 in z units. Low and high priors expect 0.25 and 2 shifted machines. An additional 15%, 40% and 65% of shifted-machine findings are assumed actionable in the three scenarios. Conditional value is the observed daily theo shortfall times post-lead days, assuming a verified fix closes the gap promptly. Do not attribute a payout difference to any cause before independent verification.

The redesigned trial would randomize every June-active tracked player as evenly as possible, stratified by tier and pre-period theo, to the current or restored tier schedules. The primary endpoint is 60-day tracked theo less economic comp cost per assigned player, analyzed intent-to-treat with a fixed CUPED adjustment using pre-period theo. The catalog specifies design, training and tracking but lists no duration cap. With a July 6 launch, August 5 is a safety and implementation look only, and September 4 is the final efficacy readout. This preserves the two-sided 5% final test. Power uses 80% power and a historical backtest: enroll April-active players, use April theo as the covariate, and follow each assigned player for the first 30 or 60 days from May 1. Retain nonreturners with zero play and keep any recorded redemption cost. The planning standard deviation is the historical outcome SD multiplied by the square root of one minus squared correlation with pre-period theo. Scale the MDE by the historical mean for the matching horizon. The June enrollment frame has 20,577 players (10,288 and 10,289 per arm). Historical CUPED planning MDEs are $4.15 (6.44%) at 30 days and $7.61 (5.77%) at 60 days. The final 60-day relative MDE must be at most 2.5% before funding, so the pilot is deferred. Neither arm adds incentives targeting heavy losers. No incremental awards are budgeted.

The comp decision value uses Jan–Apr redeemed points divided by outstanding point-days as an illustrative daily redemption hazard. Estimate the chance of use from the readout to the annual reset, assuming constant hazard and no carryover. Compare the breakage-adjusted excess economic exposure with projected theo at risk from the observed carded/uncarded differential through reset. This comparison is a scenario rather than evidence of causal lift. Low and high cases vary hazard by 30%. The conditional value credits 10%, 35% or 60% of any positive decision gap for plausible scope and implementation; the probability of a material and resolvable finding is 10%, 30% or 50%. The existing short-window redemption share is reported as an empirical check and is not interpreted as cohort breakage.

The survey would ask former and continuing High-Limit top-decile guests about visit barriers to decide whether to avoid a $15,000 promotion. Its low/base/high probabilities of changing that decision are 10%/20%/40%. Base net stays below zero. Keep it as an evaluated alternative.
