**We recommend not replacing any of the ten lowest-earning machines in July. Instead, inspect machine M-1158, hold off on the comp pilot until it can give a clear answer, and keep $98,800 of the budget in reserve.**

The fictional casino’s six-month floor recorded $248.9M in coin-in and $23.55M in theoretical win. Actual win exceeded theo by $92,791 (0.55 standard deviations), while lost operating time cost an estimated $98,064 in theo. We compared machines after accounting for downtime, zone and game configuration, then separated the remaining machine effect from payout variation. The dollar bridge reconciles to actual win. Three machines are long-term replacement candidates, with none clearing the July economics.

Back Forty weakened during the walkway closure relative to the rest of the floor, with covered-hour traffic supporting the location mechanism. A post-closure gap versus January remained; seasonal and other changes limit causal attribution. The 61-day May–June holdout brought coin-in MAE from $218,258 for a constant baseline to $55,271 for a model using weekday, holiday and announced events.

Tracked play was concentrated. The top 1% by six-month theo accounted for 22.72% of tracked theo and 22.80% of tracked coin-in and netted −$3.49M. The top 10% accounted for 66.37% and 66.33%, respectively, and netted −$10.46M. Economic comp cost was 1.23% of tracked theo. The redesigned 60-day pilot could detect a 5.77% change, above the 2.5% funding threshold. Monitor reinvestment weekly and review it if cost exceeds 1.50% of tracked theo for two complete weeks. No arm targets heavy losers.

M-1158’s payout gap is unusual after adjusting for multiple comparisons (p = 0.0015). We assign 34% to a fixable cause because accounting, a resolved issue, or a non-fixable cause could also explain the gap. The $1,200 inspection has a modeled net value of +$785 (−$682 to +$2,275 across scenarios). Verify the meter, game settings and accounting before approving further work. Preserve the remaining budget and keep the bank-position and long-term replacement tests on the general manager’s watchlist.
